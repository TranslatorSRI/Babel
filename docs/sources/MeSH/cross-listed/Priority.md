# Which pipeline owns a MeSH record that two pipelines claim

**Status: proposal, awaiting a ruling.** Nothing here is implemented yet. The work is tracked in
[#1151], stacked on [#1124]; "Picking this up" at the end says how to re-run everything from a fresh
checkout, record a ruling, and what the implementation has to touch.

MeSH files some descriptors in two trees, and each pipeline's `write_mesh_ids()` claims whole trees,
so such a descriptor lands in two pipelines' `ids/MESH` and then in two compendia. Against the
2026jul22 build that is 46 records; 20 of them led a clique in two compendia and are the rows of
`input_data/known_duplicate_clique_leaders.tsv` ([#730], [#1123]). "A UMLS CUI follows its MeSH
descriptor" ([`../../UMLS/CuiFollowsMesh.md`](../../UMLS/CuiFollowsMesh.md)) cannot settle these:
it moves CUIs to their descriptor's owner, and these descriptors have two owners. Every record and
the evidence below are in [`2026jul22.md`](2026jul22.md), generated by
`scripts/list_cross_listed_descriptors.py`.

## Why not a priority order between pipelines

Anatomy is on one side of all 46, so the obvious rule is "anatomy always loses" or "anatomy always
wins". The 2026jul22 cliques say neither works. The useful evidence is what each record's clique
held in each compendium: the side whose clique holds the record's ontology (MONDO, HP, UBERON, CL,
GO) or UMLS partners is the side that loses equivalences if it gives the record up, while a side
where the MeSH record stands alone loses nothing. By that measure anatomy should keep 24 records and
give up 22, and each pair splits both ways except anatomy and taxon:

| pipelines | records | anatomy keeps | other pipeline keeps |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| anatomy and disease | 17 | 5 | 12 |
| anatomy and protein | 14 | 5 | 9 |
| anatomy and taxon | 13 | 13 | 0 |
| anatomy and chemicals | 2 | 1 | 1 |

Centralising the tree partitions ([#735]) would not settle this either. It makes each *tree number*
belong to one pipeline, but a cross-listed descriptor has two tree numbers in two pipelines' trees,
so a perfect partition still lists it twice.

## Proposed mechanism

A committed table, `input_data/mesh_cross_listed_owners.tsv` (`curie`, `owner`, `issue`, `reason`),
applied where the `ids/MESH` files are written so that each record reaches one pipeline's
`ids/MESH`. The UMLS ownership rule then moves the record's CUIs with it, with no new UMLS code. A
record that two pipelines claim and the table does not name fails the build at that step, which
runs within minutes of `mesh.nt` downloading, so a new cross-listing in a MeSH release is decided
by a person rather than shipped. The alternative, a default order with per-record overrides, would
need a long override list for every pair but anatomy and taxon, and would decide new
cross-listings silently.

**Member-level residue.** glom() merges a concord pair whether or not an ids file lists both CURIEs,
so a record removed from the losing pipeline's `ids/MESH` stays in that pipeline's cliques as a
member if one of its concords still names it (the shape of [#1126]). The concord-edges column of
[`2026jul22.md`](2026jul22.md) lists every edge that reaches each record. Each record has at most
one UMLS edge, which follows the record's owner under "a CUI follows its MeSH descriptor". The
non-UMLS edges all sit on the proposed owner's side (MONDO and DOID mappings in disease, UBERON
xrefs in anatomy) except one: anatomy's `UBERON:0003705` "Meckel's diverticulum" xref to
`MESH:D008467`. If that record goes to disease, the pair also needs dropping from anatomy's concords
(`input_data/anatomy_badxrefs.txt`), or the record stays an anatomy member.

## Proposed owners

"Partners" is what the record's clique held in that compendium in 2026jul22; "alone" means a clique
of the MeSH record only.

### Anatomy and disease

for exactly that, `biolink:PathologicalAnatomicalStructure`, a subclass of
`biolink:AnatomicalEntity`, but it registers no identifier prefixes, so every member would need
`extra_prefixes` to survive `write_compendium()`. Retyping is out of scope here; [#456] (MPATH) is
the issue that would give that class a source.

## Picking this up

### Re-running the evidence on a fresh checkout

Everything here regenerates from a published build plus a local `mesh.nt`; nothing depends on a
local build or on this machine's `data/`. From the repository root, after `uv sync`:

1. `uv run snakemake --cores 1 get_mesh` downloads the MeSH release `config.yaml`'s `mesh_year`
   names to `babel_downloads/MESH/mesh.nt` (about 2 GB). Add `--forcerun=get_mesh` to replace an
   older download, which Snakemake otherwise reuses.
2. `docs/sources/UMLS/cui-follows-mesh/scripts/fetch_build_inputs.py --build-url <build-url>
   --output-dir data/duplicate-curies` exports the build's `ids/MESH` and `ids/UMLS` files and
   downloads its duplicate reports, in under a minute. `<build-url>` is the build's root on
   stars.renci.org; 2026jul22 is not yet the public `var/babel/latest/` (read its `VERSION.txt`).
3. `scripts/list_cross_listed_descriptors.py` (its docstring has the full command) regenerates
   [`2026jul22.md`](2026jul22.md) and [`2026jul22.csv`](2026jul22.csv) in about five minutes, most
   of it reading the build's Edge tables and `Concord.parquet` over https.

Against the same build the output should not change; against a newer build or MeSH release, compare
the record list with the tables below before ruling.

The committed tables came from 2026jul22's `ids/MESH`, which were built from the stale MeSH 2024
`mesh.nt`. Regenerated with MeSH 2026 (#1144) and every pipeline's `ids/MESH` rebuilt from it, the
output is identical, with the same 46 records, tree numbers and types, so the rulings carry over to
the first MeSH 2026 build.

### Recording a ruling

Edit the "proposed owner" cell of the record (drop "**ask**" once decided) and tick the matching
box in [#1151]'s description. A ruling that sends a record away from where its partners are should
say why in the evidence cell, because that cell becomes the `reason` column of the owners table.

### What the implementation touches

- `input_data/mesh_cross_listed_owners.tsv`, new: one row per record above (`curie`, `owner`,
  `issue`, `reason`), owner being a directory name from `config.yaml: umls_mesh_owning_pipelines`.
- `mesh.write_ids()` (`src/datahandlers/mesh.py`) and the five pipelines' `write_mesh_ids()`: drop a
  record the table gives to another pipeline.
- `umls.write_mesh_ownership()` (rule `umls_mesh_ownership`) already reads all five `ids/MESH`
  files, so it is the cheap place for the control: raise if a MeSH record is in two of them.
- Remove the 20 MeSH rows from `input_data/known_duplicate_clique_leaders.tsv` and the 46 MeSH
  entries from `KNOWN_DUPLICATES` in `tests/pipeline/test_vocabulary_partitioning.py`.
- Update `docs/sources/MeSH/Ingestion.md`, and rule 3 in
  [`CuiFollowsMesh.md`](../../UMLS/CuiFollowsMesh.md), which names these descriptors as the case
  it leaves alone.
- Confirm on a cluster build: `duplicate_clique_leaders.tsv` should have no MeSH rows, and
  `babel-clique-diff` should show each record leaving the losing compendium.
